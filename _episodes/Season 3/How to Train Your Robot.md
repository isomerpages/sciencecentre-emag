<h4><strong>Guest biography</strong></h4>
<p>Ms. Wenjun Liang is a Senior Science Educator at Science Centre Singapore.
On a daily basis, Wenjun can be found around the centre conducting shows
and interacting with guests. For the RoboFest, Wenjun has been specially
trained to work with robots.</p>
<h4><strong>Transcript</strong></h4>
<p>How To Train Your Robot</p>
<p></p>
<p>Lydia: What are some of the actions that you have taught it?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: Like, for example, if we want it to point directions to the left
or to the right, it does have those corresponding actions as well.</p>
<p>Lydia: Actually. Can we do a ‘cheers’ with it right now with our drinks?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: Oh, sure. Of course! It knows how to cheers.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Lydia: Oh, okay! You taught it already? Has it been drinking?</p>
<p>Wenjun: Uhm... (crickets).&nbsp;</p>
<p><em>Lydia, Wenjun and the Unitree G1 Humanoid Robot cheers with their Oatside drinks.</em>
</p>
<p>Lydia: Oh, not bad. This is my first time drinking (Oatside). Actually,
you can find the Oatside drinks at the RoboFestival.</p>
<p><strong>Introduction</strong>
</p>
<p>Lydia: Hello and welcome to Void Deck by Science Centre Singapore. I'm
Lydia and today we'll be talking about robots. Today we have a special
guest with us. This is the Unitree G1 humanoid robot. And of course, we
have Wenjun from the Gallery Experience team from the centre. And she's
been working very closely with these robots for the upcoming robot festival.</p>
<p>If you want to know more about the festival, do tune in to the end for
more details. But first! Wenjun, can you tell me more about the robot?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: Okay, so over here is the humanoid robot. In other words, it's
also a bipedal robot. Meaning, they are designed to walk, climb, or actually
run on two legs just like humans.</p>
<p>This robot will be joining us for RoboFest. It will be co-presenting,
as well as joining us for the actual launch event.
<br>
</p>
<p><em>For our listeners without any visual reference, we are currently featuring a life-sized robot standing at about 130cm. It has a face, arms, legs, all made of mainly lightweight, high-strength materials. You can check it out in our show notes in the link below, or head over to our YouTube channel.</em>
</p>
<div class="isomer-image-wrapper">
<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="" src="/images/robot.jpg">
</div>
<p>Lydia: Wow, is there anything else it can do?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun:It can dance as well. It can do simple moves. And of course, it’s
also able to do boxing. So you will be able to see them at our RoboFest
too.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Lydia: Wow! Wait, how can it box? Can it sense when someone is about to
punch?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: Okay, so this robot is fixed with sensors. So there's actually
a 3D lidar sensor that's able to help it to detect obstacles around, to
give it spatial awareness, as well as the camera that's also inbuilt that
is able to -- it's actually kind of like a depth camera.</p>
<p>Lydia: So this is the camera right here? <em>(points to the robot’s face)</em> There's
a lot of cameras! One, two, three, four cameras.</p>
<p>So this can withstand boxing? If someone boxed it, will it break? <em>(pokes the robot)</em> Oh,
it’s actually very sturdy and very hard.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: Yes. It's made of sturdy materials. And also, its sense of balancing
is really good. Just like us humans, right? When we feel like we are going
to fall forward or we're going to fall backwards, we are able to adjust
ourselves. So similarly for the robot, it can also adjust to try to maintain
its balance as much as possible.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Lydia: Actually, it feels very steady. How much does this weigh?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: This model that you see here is approximately 35kg.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Lydia: So all these actions that it can do, how does it learn to do them?</p>
<p>Wenjun: So some of the actions are already inbuilt. But for some of the
newer actions, or very specific actions that we need it to perform, we
can actually give it some training.</p>
<p>Okay, maybe I need the hand to move a certain way? Or within a certain
time: seconds, duration? We can give it that training as well, then it
can remember the exact movements and is able to perform those movements
again.</p>
<p>Lydia: So has there been any interesting moments when you were training
the robot?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: It's never so easy at the start when we are trying to learn how
to train it. So for example, how the joints actually move, how the weight
or the center of gravity for the robot actually shifts when we are giving
it new moves. So sometimes we realize that, when it’s trying to re-perform
the move, it starts to go a bit off balance. So that's when the self-balancing
functions for this robot comes in. Very important. It will stumble a bit
maybe, then it will start to balance back by itself.</p>
<p>Lydia: I see. So there are some restrictions as to how much it can move.
And you have to make sure it's within a certain parameter.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: Yeah. For the upper body especially.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Lydia: So what can it do for us on a day to day basis. Can it help us
with our daily lives? Can it go shopping with us? I don't know. Can it
help us with our house chores?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: Actually, this robot, I will say, even though it looks like a
human, there are still quite some limitations. For example, if you are
wiping the windows using a damp or wet cloth? Then we might need to make
sure that it doesn't interfere with the mechanisms or with the electrical
parts of the robot itself. Unlike us humans, we’re okay and perfectly fine
with coming in contact with wet cloth, with washing clothes. But not all
robots can do that.</p>
<p></p>
<p><strong>Would You Rather…</strong>
</p>
<p></p>
<p>Lydia: Would you rather have one very advanced robot or many simple robots?</p>
<p>Wenjun: Actually, I want both. Is there an option?</p>
<p>Lydia: Would you rather a robot look more robotic or look more like a
human?</p>
<p>Wenjun: Robotic.</p>
<p>Lydia: Why?</p>
<p>Wenjun: If it looks really, really like a human, then will it come to
the extent whereby people are, (like maybe for me), will I be second-guessing
what I'm seeing? Is it really a human or a robot? But I would say if a
robot really looks like a human, it does give that visual impact, that
wow factor, that ‘wow that's how much robotics have advanced over the years.’</p>
<p>Lydia: Do you actually think that this robot can replace humans?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: Not at the moment. While I will say robots are more involved in
our daily lives and we are embracing robots more and more like even our
home -- the washing machine is actually considered a robot. But I will
say in terms of replacing humans, it will be a no. Because although AI
is on the rise, I think there's still a difference between a human brain
and an AI brain.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Lydia: Cause for this one anyway, you program it right? You need a human
to program this. Okay, so for the upcoming robot festival, do you have
any more information about that?</p>
<p>Wenjun: It's going to be exciting.</p>
<p>Lydia: So other than this, will there be other robots as well? Other types
of robots?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: Yes. We also have a robot dog that will make its appearance for
one of our shows as well. Maybe you will encounter some robots that are
able to serve you something.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Lydia: Serve us something? Serve us food?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Wenjun: Edible stuff.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Lydia: Wow! Okay. I think I'm quite excited for this.</p>
<p>So our robot festival is happening from 9 to 12 April during our normal
operating hours. So do come down and join us if you're interested in looking
at our robots and interacting with them.</p>
